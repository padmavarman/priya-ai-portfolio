Professor Predict: How LLMs Learn

An interactive exercise pairing with a generative AI chatbot to explore how large language models are trained, and where their answers can and cannot be trusted.

Introduction Description Objective Process Tools Value Proposition

Introduction

For this artifact, I worked through an interactive course exercise called "Professor Predict," pairing one-on-one with a generative AI chatbot acting as a guide rather than a search engine. The goal was to understand, hands-on, how large language models actually generate answers, and why that process doesn't guarantee the answer is true.

Description

The exercise moved through a series of stages, each building on the last:

  • Be the Model: I completed familiar phrases ("The capital of France is ___," "Once upon a ___") the way a language model would, then hit a trick question about the president of a fictional country, Velmora, which doesn't exist.
  • Reveal the Mechanism: The chatbot explained how models are trained to predict the next likely word based on patterns, not a guaranteed fact-check.
  • Guess the Training Mix: I sorted different kinds of information (public articles, private messages, live data) by how likely they were to appear in a model's training data.
  • Good Fit / Danger Zone: I classified real tasks, like drafting a cover letter versus giving a current stock price, as either safe for AI to handle alone or requiring a source, tool, or human check.
  • Completion Summary: The chatbot generated a personalized summary of my responses and reasoning throughout the exercise.

Objective

The objective was to build practical judgment about when an AI-generated answer can be trusted versus when it needs independent verification, understanding that a model produces a plausible-sounding continuation based on learned patterns, not an automatically verified fact.

Process

I copied the exercise's structured prompt into a commercial AI chatbot and worked through the conversation genuinely, answering each stage's questions myself rather than skipping ahead. When I hit the Velmora question, I had to decide in the moment whether to guess, and recognizing that I had no real grounding for an answer was the most useful part of the exercise. I carried that same instinct into the later stages, sorting training data categories and classifying tasks as a Good Fit or Danger Zone, before receiving a personalized completion summary at the end.

"I thought this would just be a fill-in-the-blank game… Then I got asked who the president of Velmora is, and I honestly had nothing, so I passed. Turns out Velmora isn't even real. That was the whole point. A model doesn't stop when it has nothing to go on."

Tools and Technologies Used

  • A commercial generative AI chatbot — used as the interactive guide, following a structured instructional prompt provided in the course
  • A written reflection, completed afterward to connect the exercise back to my own reasoning and takeaways

Value Proposition

🧭

Unique Value

I can critically evaluate AI-generated output rather than just use AI tools passively, telling the difference between an answer that sounds fluent and one that's actually verified.

🎯

Relevance

Knowing when to trust an AI answer versus when to verify it independently is an essential, practical skill for using these tools responsibly in a business or banking setting.
